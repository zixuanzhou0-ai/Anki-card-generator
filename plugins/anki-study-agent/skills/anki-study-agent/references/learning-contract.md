# Learning contract

## First principle

A card is a repeatable, scoreable retrieval event:

`cue → learner attempt → explicit scoring boundary → accurate feedback → later spaced retrieval`

A candidate is worth a card only when:

- it supports a future behavior the learner values;
- the cue is sufficient without leaking the answer;
- the core answer is scoreable;
- feedback is supported by source evidence or a labeled explanation source;
- spaced retrieval is the right practice format;
- expected learning value exceeds future review debt.

“One card, one target” means one scoring boundary. A grammar frame, causal chain, or decision rule may be one target when it can be judged as one response.

## Hard gates

Reject, split, downgrade, or convert a candidate to a note/practice task when any gate fails:

- evidence cannot be replayed;
- it does not serve the learning contract;
- it is already known or duplicates another target;
- the answer boundary is ambiguous;
- source claims conflict without a resolvable scope, time, or attribution;
- the material is unauthorized or sensitive;
- review cost outweighs likely benefit.

Model confidence never overrides a failed gate.

## Ranking and portfolio

Explain recommendations using goal relevance, future frequency or stakes, transfer and bottleneck value, forgetting or confusion risk, evidence confidence, learner fit, scoreability, and review cost.

Select a portfolio, not raw top-N. Balance routes, difficulty, prerequisites, recognition, production, transfer, and contrast while limiting near-duplicates.

## Routes

For language material, distinguish reading/listening recognition, active production, grammar cloze, pronunciation, pragmatics/register, chunks/collocations, and contrast. Verify exact spans and media timing; never feed display HTML to TTS.

For general knowledge, identify the knowledge type before choosing a card: stable fact, discriminating definition, mechanism, causal relation, procedure, decision rule, comparison, misconception, or source disagreement. Preserve qualifiers, scope, time, units, and attribution.

Use an independent review role only for high-value disagreement, conflicts, complex pragmatics or causality, anomalous validation, or benchmark evaluation. A committee cannot vote unsupported claims into truth.
